# What Second Look can and cannot claim

Status: in development, 28 Oct 2026. The allowed claims come from the build plan (`PLAN.md`): "reads signed provenance; flags concerns with reasons; can't tell; earliest copy found." Only the first three exist in the code today.

## What we cannot claim

Second Look must not say any of these, in the product, the docs, the video or the post:

- That it can tell AI images from camera images, or that it identifies AI-made images in general. It reads signed records and flags concerns. A concern is a reason to look closer.
- That it is correct some percentage of the time. Rates are published only as an observed rate with an upper bound, per arm, per run, on a stated test set.
- That an image is genuine, authentic, or safe to trust. `signed_camera` means a signer's record says a camera captured it. `cant_tell` means we found nothing either way.
- That a signed record proves a claim about the picture. A valid manifest says how a signer says the file was made. It does not say what the image shows is true, or that the scene was not staged.
- That the classifier works on generators, formats or edits outside the test set. The first test set uses one generator family (see "Known limits").
- That it is a fact-checker. The claim rung gives no verdict of its own. It quotes someone else's.
- That a model explains or writes up a result. No model writes explanations; model readings are shown only as labelled readings. Glimmer's outputs are labelled readings and verbatim search clauses only, and the plain-language explanation comes from fixed templates, with no model (E1, merged at `e24ad30`).
- That it is made by, endorsed by or affiliated with Meta.
- That the `likely_fine` band is at most 1% AI, or any "1%" figure for that side. The held-out result is 3 of 326, with an upper bound of 2.36%. The 1% target was a dev-fitted operating point. The 0.95% bound covers camera photos in `auto_decided` only.
- That Second Look "detects AI", or that `auto_decided` is a verdict. The bands route images to a person or to spot checks. `likely_fine` is not proof that an image is real.
- That the queue results hold on a desk's own folder, on other generators or on other platforms. They come from one public set of Facebook-shared copies, and all 3 `likely_fine` misses are SD 3.
- That the queue means the classifier gate passed. The 29 Oct gate failed. The queue's thresholds are a separate, dev-fitted routing point.
- That the shipped ladder catches AI images at scale. On 983 TrueFake files it answered `cant_tell` on all of them ([FAILURES.md](FAILURES.md)).

## Known limits

These are written down so no one has to find them.

**Signed answers**

- **Trust list.** At the C2PA rung's first review (stage 1), no trust list of accepted signers was loaded. A manifest with a valid signature from a self-issued certificate could be read as valid. The reviewer's top finding was that the ladder must require a trusted signer before it gives `signed_ai` or `signed_camera`. The fix round (`s2-rungs` commit `f3048d1`, merged to main at `b4c160d`) loads the C2PA Trust List and requires a valid and trusted manifest for the "signed" strength.
- **Signature failures are listed, not ignored.** One public sample from the IPTC (`iptc-board-signed.jpg`) fails signature validation under `c2pa-python` 0.38.2 (`claimSignature.mismatch`, signer untrusted). The rung reports it as an invalid manifest and the ladder lists it as a concern, not as a signed answer.
- **A manifest held only in the cloud** (a remote manifest reference with nothing embedded) is not fetched or read, because Second Look works offline. The c2pa rung reports it in a card ("stored online ... did not fetch or check it"), which is not a concern, and the answer stays `cant_tell` (stage 2b ruling).
- **Stripped files.** Signed provenance is easy to remove by re-saving or screenshotting. Such an image gets `cant_tell`, which is what it should get.

**Concerns**

- Unsigned metadata can be written or removed by anyone, so it is a reason and never a verdict.
- The classifier reports a score. Its limit is stated on every card: it can be wrong, especially on generators it never saw, after heavy compression, on screenshots and on edited photos. A low score is not evidence of a camera.
- The classifier's threshold is chosen on a separate development split, with the file and its sha256 recorded, before the test images are scored.

**The test set** (design notes from the L4 build, 28 Oct 2026)

- **One generator family.** The AI images come from DiffusionDB (CC0), which is Stable Diffusion 1.x output through one public Discord bot, dated 6 to 20 August 2022. A pass on this set says nothing about other generators or newer versions.
- **The classifier was trained on the same generator family.** Community Forensics (Park and Owens, CVPR 2025) trains on images from 4,763 Hugging Face models, and they include the Stable Diffusion 1.x checkpoints themselves (v1-1 to v1-4 and v1-5). So the AI side of the gate tests a family the detector has seen. It is not a held-out-generator test. DiffusionDB's own images are not in its training data, because the authors generated their own. None of our Commons photos is in its released LAION training list. Its real training data (LAION, ImageNet and nine others) and its LAION-2B pretraining are web-scale, so overlap with the kind of photographs we use is possible but not measured. Statement, sources and the check: [TRAINING-DATA.md](TRAINING-DATA.md) (merged at `d842651`). The threshold file carries the statement, and `calibrate freeze` refuses to run without it. Any catch-rate figure carries this label: "training-overlap: same generator family (Stable Diffusion 1.x is in Community Forensics' training set)".
- **Held-out-generator arm: reported, not gated.** A separate arm uses generators held out of the detector's training by its authors (their public evaluation set): Community Forensics `publicEval`, CC BY 4.0, AI side only (DAT-848 `318586c2`). It is reported as observed, with its bound, and is not part of the pass rule. It ran on 29 Oct 2026 (AEDT) on 300 images from 18 generators and caught 263/300 in `original`, 206/300 in `format_matched` and 166/300 in `compressed_q70_1080` (one-sided 95% lower bounds 84.096%, 63.970% and 50.422%); see [RESULTS-heldout.md](RESULTS-heldout.md).
- **Curated non-AI photos.** They come from Wikimedia Commons categories reviewed for sharpness and composition, uploaded between 2004 and 2021. Everyday images are messier, and none of these shows newer phone computational photography.
- **Format and size confound.** The non-AI set is mostly large JPEGs with camera metadata. The AI set is mostly 512 by 512 PNG with none. A classifier could score on that alone, so every image goes through one frozen preprocessing recipe, and a file-facts-only baseline is added as a check.
- **People: screened on 28 and 29 Oct 2026 (AEDT); not certified person-free.** Every fetched image, real and AI, went through OpenCV stage-1 triage; only images it flagged went through the local Muse Glimmer stage-2 check. Stage 1 is OpenCV triage: three Haar face cascades and the HOG people detector, at high-recall settings. Stage 2 is a local Muse Glimmer 30B check of the images stage 1 flagged, and of those only. It was asked "Is any person, face or human body part visible in this image? Answer yes, no or unsure." Under the frozen answer rule (D3, restored on 29 Oct at 08:04 AEDT, `ERRATA-v2.md` in the L4 study folder), exactly "no" accepts an image; "yes", "unsure" and any other reply exclude it. Animal faces are allowed. Dated runs: dev on 28 Oct, 20:00 to 22:29; test on 29 Oct, 01:45 to 04:37; the v2 top-up on 29 Oct, 05:03 to 05:26; the held-out arm on 29 Oct, 06:06 to 07:43; then every stored reply was re-read under the frozen rule and the reserve walks continued (v1 at 12:08 to 13:04, the top-up at 14:41 to 14:45). Counts under that rule: the v1 set kept 88 real and 120 AI dev images and 383 real and 293 AI test images, and the people check excluded 227 images (each one flagged by stage 1) while 690 stage-2 replies were applied; the v2 top-up added 37 real dev and 42 real test images, excluding 34 at that check on 101 replies; the joined v2 set is dev 125 real and 120 AI, test 425 real and 293 AI, and the gate reports 261 people-check exclusions across both; the held-out arm screened 612 candidates, excluded 257 at that check and took 300 of the 355 that passed. Sources: `SCREEN-CONFIG.json`, `SCREEN-RESULT-v1-final-D3.md`, `ACCEPTED-v1-final-D3.json`, `ACCEPTED-v2-topup-D3.json`, `ACCEPTED-v2-D3.json`, `heldout-v2/ACCEPTED-heldout-D3.json` and the gate's [RESULTS-v2.md](RESULTS-v2.md). Limits: stage 2 sees only what stage 1 flagged, so a person the old detectors miss (small, turned away, partly hidden or drawn) is never checked by stage 2. The Muse Glimmer reply is a model reading, not evidence, and it can say "no" about an image that shows a person. This screen does not certify a person-free or eligible pool; the accepted images are not certified person-free (DAT-849).
- **Pool size.** The v1 run was below the dev floor: 88 real dev units against 100 under the screening rule in force (D3). [RESULTS-v1.md](RESULTS-v1.md) reports 96, from a parser fix (D1′) that was later not accepted; both are below 100 ([RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md)). It is reported as INVALID, not as pass or fail. The v2 run met the floors: dev 125 real and 120 AI, test 425 real and 293 AI ([RESULTS-v2.md](RESULTS-v2.md)).

**The context rung**

- It compares metadata, and metadata can be wrong, edited or stripped. A disagreement does not show the picture is fake, and agreement doesn't show how the picture was made. Every context card says so.
- The rules are fixed in the code: dates more than 2 days apart, a place the GPS position is clearly outside (below), a device make that is clearly different. A camera clock that was never set, or a phone that kept its home time zone, will read as a date disagreement.
- Places come from GeoNames: places of 5,000 people or more, the country table and first-level regions (states, provinces). A caption's place is read as a country or region first. "Taken in Australia" disagrees only when the GPS position is within 25 km of a listed place outside Australia and the nearest listed place in Australia is more than 50 km farther away. There are no borders in the data: a point by a border counts as in or near both sides, and a point in empty country or at sea, with no listed place within 25 km, is not placed at all.
- A city counts only when its name is unambiguous: the largest place of that name has at least 10 times the people of the next, or the caption's country or region leaves one ("Paris, Texas"). It disagrees when it is more than 50 km away and the GPS position is clearly in another first-level region. A name that fits any listed place near the GPS position agrees. An ambiguous name ("Springfield", "Victoria", "Rome", which is also another name of Lomé) is not compared, and the card says so. A wrong caption can therefore pass; a truthful one should not raise a concern.
- The caption reader is rule-based. It reads the first full date with a year in the caption (so "unveiled on 12 May 2019" is read as the date, and the card says it is the first full date in the caption), a capitalised place after "in", "at", "near" or "outside" (or "from" when none of those is followed by a place), and a device after "shot on", "taken with" and the like. It skips what it cannot read for sure ("yesterday", or an all-number date such as 03/04/2026 that reads day first or month first). When the first date in the caption is such an all-number date, no date is kept, a later date is not taken in its place, and the card says the date was not compared (DAT-873). Typed facts (`--claimed-date`, `--claimed-place`, `--claimed-device`) override it.
- The GPS position is never copied into the result. Only the distance is.

**The claim rung**

- It matches words. It does not understand meaning, and it ignores negation words, so a claim and its opposite can match. Such a match carries a flag and its card says so.
- The feed holds what fact-checkers chose to publish. "No existing fact-check found" says nothing about whether a claim is true.
- Informal checks on 28 Oct (300 claims quoted from the feed, 295 of 300 found with about a quarter of the words dropped) show that the matcher finds a review whose wording it is given. They do not show how often a user's own claim has an existing fact-check, and they are not a gate.

## The gate, in one place

Defined before any test image is scored (locked in DAT-795):

- Threshold frozen on a development set of at least 100 non-AI and 100 AI images.
- Test set of 400 to 600 independent non-AI images and 200 AI images, one test row per source group.
- Three arms reported separately: originals, format-matched copies (JPEG q90, 1,536 px), compressed copies (JPEG q70, long side 1,080 px). The original and format-matched arms are the gated arms. The compressed arm and the held-out-generator arm (see "The test set") are reported, not gated.
- **Pass** needs both: the one-sided 95% exact (Clopper-Pearson) upper bound on false positives is at most 2%, and the lower bound on AI images caught is at least 40%.
- Every rate is reported as observed, with its bound. The output never states "under 2%" as a finding.
- A failing or invalid run is published as it stands. The floors are not lowered.
